# Enterprise Information Security Policy

Document ID: SEC-POL-001
Version: 4.2
Classification: Internal
Effective Date: 2026-07-01
Policy Owner: Information Security Office
Review Cycle: Annual

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes minimum information-security requirements for employees, contractors, applications, infrastructure, and corporate data.

The objective is to protect organizational information against unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, destruction, and service disruption.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

* Corporate employees and contractors
* Production and development environments
* Corporate endpoints and mobile devices
* Cloud infrastructure
* Business applications
* Customer and company information
* Third-party systems processing company data

## 3. Access Management

Access must be granted according to the principle of least privilege.

All privileged accounts must use multi-factor authentication. Shared administrative accounts are prohibited unless specifically approved by Information Security.

User access must be reviewed periodically and removed when employment or contractual access ends.

## 4. Data Classification

Information must be classified into one of the following categories:

PUBLIC
Information approved for unrestricted distribution.

INTERNAL
Information intended for authorized personnel and normal business operations.

CONFIDENTIAL
Business-sensitive information requiring controlled access.

RESTRICTED
Highly sensitive information requiring explicit authorization and additional security controls.

## 5. Password and Authentication Requirements

Passwords must meet the organization's minimum complexity requirements and must not be reused across corporate and personal services.

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for administrative access and externally accessible corporate applications where supported.

Authentication credentials must never be stored in source code, tickets, documentation, or unsecured files.

## 6. Security Incident Reporting

Employees must report suspected security incidents to the Security Operations team as soon as reasonably possible.

Examples include:

* Suspected credential compromise
* Malware detection
* Unauthorized access
* Accidental disclosure of confidential information
* Lost corporate devices
* Suspicious email or login activity

## 7. Logging and Monitoring

Production systems must maintain security-relevant audit logs appropriate to their risk classification.

Logs must be protected against unauthorized modification and retained according to applicable retention requirements.

## 8. Exceptions

Policy exceptions require documented business justification, risk assessment, compensating controls, and approval from the designated security authority.

## 9. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in access restrictions, mandatory remediation, disciplinary action, contractual remedies, or other measures permitted by applicable organizational policies.
